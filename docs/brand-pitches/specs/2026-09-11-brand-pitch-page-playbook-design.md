# Brand Pitch Page Playbook Design

## Goal

Create a reusable PixVerse playbook for building customer-specific brand pitch pages. The guidance must apply across categories while preserving the strongest lessons from the REVOLVE pitch-page iterations.

## Audience

- PixVerse sales, growth, creative, product marketing, and solution teams.
- Agents or contractors producing pitch pages on PixVerse's behalf.
- Reviewers responsible for brand accuracy, demo quality, or publication.

## Deliverable

Publish one practical Markdown playbook at `docs/brand-pitches/playbook.md`. It will be organized as a six-stage workflow:

1. Strategy
2. Research
3. Demo curation
4. Page design
5. Quality assurance
6. Publish and measure

The playbook will also include reusable templates for the pitch brief, product-truth record, demo scorecard, and pre-publish review.

## Principles

- Show customer-specific proof before explaining the technology.
- Design for one audience, one decision, and one primary action.
- Treat customer facts, creative interpretation, and hypotheses as separate information classes.
- Use official brand assets and customer visual language without implying an unapproved partnership.
- Keep generation mechanics, secrets, internal identifiers, and operational language out of the customer experience.
- Make desktop and mobile compositions intentional, fast, accessible, and resilient.
- Use measurable pilot criteria that the customer can validate.

## Brand-Agnostic Scope

- Use REVOLVE only as a lessons-learned case study, not as the template's assumed industry.
- Express product-fidelity checks by category so the process covers apparel, hardware, beauty, packaged goods, and other physical products.
- Keep exact commercial terms, SKU counts, durations, and quality thresholds configurable through the pitch brief.

## Verification

- The playbook contains no credentials, private URLs, local paths, or unsupported customer claims.
- Every required section and reusable template is complete, with no placeholders presented as facts.
- Design guidance covers official logos, co-brand lockups, favicon/app icons, social previews, demo framing, responsive behavior, and visual anti-patterns.
- Operational guidance covers performance, accessibility, security, analytics, staging, production verification, maintenance, and rollback.
- The documentation change remains scoped to the reusable playbook and its design rationale, without unrelated product or deployment changes.
